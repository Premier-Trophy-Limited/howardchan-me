regulated buyers want measurable outcomes and safety controls before adoption. the requirement is tied to whether a product can show results and operate safely in a school setting.

that changes the conversation around AI and edtech from interest in what is new to confidence in what has been tested.

novelty gets you noticed. it can create initial attention around a product or trial.

evidence gets you through the door. measurable outcomes and safety controls give regulated buyers the basis for adoption.

the transferable lesson is direct: build for evidence and safety alongside the product itself. a novel idea may start the conversation, while proof and controls support the next conversation with a regulated buyer.

the funding signal points toward AI and edtech trials where outcomes can be measured and safety can be addressed clearly.
